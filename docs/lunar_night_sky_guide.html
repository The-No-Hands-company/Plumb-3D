The Secret of Planetary 3D Graphics

In real-world visual effects (even in space movies and NASA documentaries), 3D artists do not sculpt individual craters by hand. The moon is a clean, mathematically smooth sphere. Its stunning reality comes from surface texturing, grazing sunlight, and telephoto lens compression. By mastering these three elements, you can render almost anything in 3D.

STEP 01

The Celestial Sphere

Primitives & Navigation

In Plumb 3D, open the Welcome Hub and click 🌕 Lunar Night Sky (or from the top menu, click Help → 🌕 Lunar Night Sky Walkthrough). Plumb 3D places a perfect, smooth UV Sphere in the center of your 3D universe.

Orbit Camera: Hold Middle Mouse Button or Alt + Left Click and drag.
Pan Camera: Hold Shift + Middle Mouse Button.
Zoom In/Out: Scroll the Mouse Wheel.
Center Focus: Press Numpad . to frame the sphere directly in center view.
STEP 02

Lunar Regolith Material

PBR Shading

The surface detail comes from the Lunar Regolith Material. The moon's surface is made of fine pulverized rock and glass beads with no shiny plastic gloss:

Base Color & Albedo
Silvery-ash gray. The real moon is actually quite dark, reflecting only ~12% of incident sunlight.
Roughness (0.85)
High roughness eliminates artificial plastic glares and gives the soft matte moon look.
Pattern 5 (Basalt Maria)
Procedural turbulent noise automatically creates the dark volcanic seas and bright highlands.
STEP 03

Directing Sunlight & The Terminator Line

Lighting & Lunar Phases

The moon does not emit light; it is a mirror reflecting the Sun. The boundary where daylight ends and night begins is called the Terminator Line:

🌕 The Glowing Full Moon

The Sun is positioned almost directly in front of the moon (near the camera). The entire visible disk is illuminated with maximum brilliance against the black space background.

Walkthrough HUD → Click "🌕 Full Moon Sunlight"
🌙 The Dramatic Crescent

The Sun is moved to the side at a 90° angle. The grazing light casts long, deep shadows across the surface, giving dramatic depth and showcasing the 3D curvature.

Walkthrough HUD → Click "🌙 Crescent Moon Sunlight"
STEP 04

Astrophotography Telephoto Framing

Camera & Optics

Why does the moon look so tiny when you take a photo with a smartphone? Because smartphones have wide-angle lenses! Professional astrophotographers use telephoto lenses (200mm–400mm) that compress perspective:

• Field of View (FOV): 22° (Equivalent to a 180mm telephoto lens)
• Depth of Field (DoF): Focus Distance 16.0m, f/4.0 aperture
• Result: The moon feels monumental and towering in the sky!
STEP 05

Render Your Masterpiece

Monte Carlo Path Tracer

Press F12 or click "Render Lunar Night Sky" on the walkthrough HUD. The offline production path tracer will immediately compute physical light transport:

✔ Progressive Accumulation: Watch the image sharpen sample-by-sample in real time.
✔ Atmospheric Bloom Halo: The bright lunar silhouette automatically produces a soft radiant glow against deep space.
✔ ACES Filmic Tone Mapping: Preserves highlights without clipping or burning out the lunar maria.
💾 When the render reaches your target samples (e.g. 64 or 128 samples), click "Save Image as PNG" in the Render window to save your wallpaper-worthy artwork!
